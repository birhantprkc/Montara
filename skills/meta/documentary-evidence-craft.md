# Montara Documentary Evidence Craft

A genre extension of [craft.md](craft.md) for documentary, factual explainer,
geopolitical, historical, educational, and data-led videos. Use it whenever the
audience will treat visuals, maps, charts, or captions as evidence.

This skill generalizes the premium documentary method that Montara previously
called "Warfront craft." Keep the method; remove the brand dependency.

## Load With

- `skills/meta/craft.md`

## Evidence Rules

1. **Every claim needs a source or a confidence label.** A source-backed claim
   may be shown assertively. An uncertain claim must be labeled as estimated,
   disputed, reported, or illustrative.
2. **Maps must be honest.** Do not draw precise borders, arrows, routes,
   heatmaps, or control zones unless the data supports that precision.
3. **Visual authority must match factual confidence.** A beautiful graphic can
   become misinformation if it implies certainty the research does not have.
4. **Do not overpromise in thumbnails or cold opens.** The hook can be sharp,
   but the finished video must deliver the promised insight.
5. **Keep a claim ledger.** Record important on-screen assertions with source,
   confidence, and the scene where the claim appears.

## Premium Documentary Method

### Voice

Voice is measurable, not "vibe." Profile a reference narrator or target style
with `hear` when possible:

- pace and pauses;
- onset density;
- warmth/centroid;
- loudness and dynamic range;
- emotional register by scene.

Target pace should match the chosen piece. The often-cited documentary target
around 4.4 onsets/second is a useful reference, not a hardcoded default.

### Music

Music is a scene-mapped score, not a flat bed:

- cue sheet per scene: `start`, `end`, `fadeIn`, `fadeOut`, `gain`;
- intentional silence before big names, numbers, reveals, or moral turns;
- crossfade loops when extending beds;
- no hard `aloop` seams that click;
- voice intelligibility wins over music energy.

### Mastering

Use one measured master pass:

- one gentle duck/sidechain if needed;
- one loudness normalization pass;
- social/YouTube default: `-14 LUFS / -1 dBTP / 48 kHz`;
- keep a lossless intermediate for revision.

### Cold Open

Move from frame 1. Good factual cold opens include:

- a map push that reveals the question;
- a real source moment with immediate motion;
- a document or dashboard reveal;
- a data animation that changes within the first second;
- a contradiction between narration and image that resolves later.

Do not open on dead b-roll unless stillness is a deliberate dramatic choice.

### Visual Grammar

Prefer visuals that help the viewer understand the specific subject:

- maps when location relationships matter;
- timelines when sequence and causality matter;
- annotated documents when proof matters;
- comparison cards when tradeoffs matter;
- system diagrams when mechanics matter;
- source footage when authenticity matters.

Do not apply generic cards, fixed colors, fixed CTAs, or fixed timing beats.

## Shorts And Reels

Shorts must be transcript-cut:

- choose cut points on sentence or clause boundaries;
- verify cuts against timed transcript;
- preserve context around names, numbers, and quotes;
- do not guess a cut because "the last three seconds should be the ending."

## QA Checklist

- Does every important claim have a source/confidence note?
- Does every map or chart avoid unsupported precision?
- Does the first second move?
- Does the music leave space for the strongest evidence?
- Does text survive phone viewing?
- Does the master hit the loudness target without pumping?
- Did someone review full playback, not just still frames?
- Are Shorts cut on transcript boundaries?
- Are thumbnails distinct and truthful?
